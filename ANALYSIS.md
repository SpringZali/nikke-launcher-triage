## Method and limits

- Read the report, all 12 revisions and the comments. Revision 13 (12:17 GMT) was compared with revision 12 at 12:45 GMT, and revision 17 (14:04 GMT) with revision 13 at 14:10 GMT: the only net change is a new box at the top (row 29). Revision 18 (14:30 GMT) only adds translations.
- Re-checked what can be re-checked from public sources: domain registries, certificate logs, public DNS, and the official CDN.
- **Did not** contact the attacker's servers, fetch the payload, or run anything from the report. Claims that need that are marked NOT VERIFIED on purpose.
- Did not examine victims' raw data. Comment-based claims are only as good as the comments.
- Scanned the comments by keyword for reports of stolen data being misused and of logs being edited (rows 21 and 22). A keyword scan can miss phrasing.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = reproduced here from a public source. **CORROBORATED** = several independent reports agree, not reproduced here. **REPORTED** = one source. **NOT VERIFIED** = could not or chose not to check. **OUTDATED** = was right when written, no longer is. **DERIVED** = computed from other rows. **REASONED** = follows from documented capabilities, not observed. **NO REPORTS FOUND** = searched for, nothing found. **NOT SUPPORTED** = checked, and the evidence points the other way. **UNOFFICIAL** = a claim or advice from the community report that the publisher does not make.

## Source priority

Since the publisher's guidance came out (Nov 4, 23:00 GMT), the order is: (1) the publisher's notices, (2) our own checks of public data, (3) the community report and its comments. The report was written by a private individual. The publisher has not confirmed its analysis of the payload. A security vendor's news article (Malwarebytes, Nov 5) repeats it but cites the report instead of confirming it, and BleepingComputer (Nov 4) confirms two parts from its own examination: the hidden loader in two of the modified files and wallet-stealing code in a DLL sample (row 37). We did not verify the payload analysis. Where the report and the publisher differ, we follow the publisher. Where the report covers cases that the official steps miss (the Oct 23 build, the HMT and Google Play Games folders, Linux through Proton, a second Windows account, what to copy before a clean-up), the README adds them as marked notes. Its steps stay the publisher's.

## Reproduce it

Use `curl.exe` (Windows 10/11) or any HTTP client. **Do not open `insightjs` addresses in a browser**: use DNS-over-HTTPS and registries only.

```
curl.exe -s https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/insightjs.com
curl.exe -s https://rdap.publicinterestregistry.org/rdap/domain/insightjs.org
curl.exe -s "https://api.certspotter.com/v1/issuances?domain=insightjs.com&include_subdomains=true&expand=dns_names&expand=issuer"
curl.exe -s -H "accept: application/dns-json" "https://cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query?name=insightjs.com&type=A"
curl.exe -s "https://api.github.com/gists/d6c049a09ba8ac3085c51ee9df24a4e5/comments?per_page=100"
```

Pin one CDN server and read the headers (change the IP to test another server). Expected clean `ETag` for this file: `04ee9800128396ba26f10ca70f170967`. The modified version had `b7d842200372ad0d9a84ef4cc8511311`.

```
curl.exe -s -D - -o NUL --resolve na-webassets.playerinfinite.com:443:43.175.144.148 https://na-webassets.playerinfinite.com/comm/nikke_launcher/assets/FeedList.feb647c9.js
```
