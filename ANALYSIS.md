## Method and limits

- Read the report, all 12 revisions and the comments. Revision 13 (12:17 GMT) was compared with revision 12 at 12:45 GMT, and revision 17 (14:04 GMT) with revision 13 at 14:10 GMT: the only net change is a new box at the top (row 29). Revision 18 (14:30 GMT) only adds translations.
- Re-checked what can be re-checked from public sources: domain registries, certificate logs, public DNS, and the official CDN.
- **Did not** contact the attacker's servers, fetch the payload, or run anything from the report. Claims that need that are marked NOT VERIFIED on purpose.
- Did not examine victims' raw data. Comment-based claims are only as good as the comments.
- Scanned the comments by keyword for reports of stolen data being misused and of logs being edited (rows 21 and 22). A keyword scan can miss phrasing.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = reproduced here from a public source. **CORROBORATED** = several independent reports agree, not reproduced here. **REPORTED** = one source. **NOT VERIFIED** = could not or chose not to check. **OUTDATED** = was right when written, no longer is. **DERIVED** = computed from other rows. **REASONED** = follows from documented capabilities, not observed. **NO REPORTS FOUND** = searched for, nothing found. **NOT SUPPORTED** = checked, and the evidence points the other way. **UNOFFICIAL** = a claim or advice from the community report that the publisher does not make.

## Source priority

Since the publisher's guidance came out (Nov 4, 23:00 GMT), the order is: (1) the publisher's notices, (2) our own checks of public data, (3) the community report and its comments. The report was written by a private individual. The publisher has not confirmed its analysis of the payload, a security vendor's news article (Malwarebytes, Nov 5) repeats it but cites the report instead of confirming it (row 37), and we did not verify it. Where the report and the publisher differ, we follow the publisher, and the README does not base advice on the report.

## About the author and the report

- GitHub account created 2025-10-02, 70 public repositories, 21 followers, 2 public gists. Not a brand-new account.
- 12 public revisions with visible corrections (for example the withdrawn `onominan.tmp` marker and the `creds` correction). The "Reinstalling Windows is the safest option" line is identical in all 18 revisions, and sits under "If you are infected".
- A private individual, not the publisher and not a security vendor. The only security vendor coverage we found (Malwarebytes, Nov 5, row 37) cites the report's analysis instead of confirming it independently, and we did not verify it. We use the report for leads and recheck what can be rechecked (rows 2 to 8).
- Several commenters say they used AI assistants to analyse their own machines. That does not change whether a claim is true. Hashes, timestamps and log lines can be checked either way.

## Where the report is strong

- It separates what it checked from what was reported by others ("reported" is used consistently).
- It corrects itself in public and keeps the revision history.
- Every checkable fact that was tested matched, with two small exceptions (rows 9 and 10), both of which came from events after it was written.
- That covers the public facts we could recheck. Its analysis of the payload and its advice are not verified, and the publisher has not confirmed them.

## What a better guide should do differently

These are suggestions for the report, not criticism of the analysis:

1. **Put the decision path first.** One line under "If you are infected" gets quoted without its condition. People who never had the agent are wiping their PCs, and several commenters say they nuked everything before checking the log.
2. **Say "check before you uninstall" as step 0.** The report notes that the log is gone after a reinstall, but only in a status paragraph, not in "What to do".
3. **Separate four things:** exposed, loader ran, agent ran, data was taken. Right now "who is at risk" covers everyone who launched, while the report itself says the payload came on only some launches.
4. **Move the changelog out of the header.** The paragraph under the title has seven "earlier" notes before the reader gets to the advice.
5. **Update the two outdated lines** (rows 9 and 10).
